# Cash payments require an open shift; bank transfer doesn't

Cash reconciliation (`ShiftService.GetReconciliation`) sums cash payments stamped with a shift's `ShiftId` against the counted `OpeningCash`. Before this decision, `PaymentService.Checkout` let any payment through with `ShiftId = null` when the cashier had no open shift (`PaymentServiceTests.Checkout_AllowsPayment_WhenCashierHasNoOpenShift` locked this in) — the only signal was a passive orange warning on `PaymentWindow`, read once in the view model's constructor, so it went stale the moment a shift opened or closed in another window.

Cash physically touches the drawer being reconciled at end of shift; bank transfer doesn't. Gating every payment method on an open shift (as Odoo's Point of Sale session does) would block a legitimate bank-transfer sale for no operational reason. Gating none of them leaves cash payments with no shift to reconcile against — a hole a real cashier would exploit the same way restaurant POS fraud usually does (skip the shift, pocket cash, nothing to compare against at close-out).

Decision: `PaymentService.Checkout` rejects `PaymentMethod.Cash` with `CheckoutResult.NoOpenShift` when the cashier has no open shift; `PaymentMethod.BankTransfer` is unaffected. Enforced in the Service layer (not the ViewModel/UI), so it can't go stale — the Service is the single source of truth. `MainWindow`'s status bar (see the `MainWindowViewModel.ShiftStatusText` change in the same PR) is the primary way a cashier discovers they need to open a shift; `PaymentWindow`'s orange warning is now just a hint pointing at the same rule.
